# #00295 -- Bring the research pipeline back

**Type:** question | **Priority:** Medium | **Status:** `BLOCKED: owner decision on the scope`

Owner decision 15 (#00287): the seven research cron jobs on the host should work again instead of being removed. This todo holds the scope and a plan. Nothing is built, and nothing on the host is changed, before the owner has chosen.

Scoped on 2026-11-15, read-only: the repo and its history, the host, and the local copy of the database.

**What the pipeline was.** A study of chat quality in the main chat channel, built on 2026-04-30 (`c9e62836`). The bot copied every message of that channel into `general_messages_raw`, and seven cron jobs worked from that table:

- `build-context-incremental` stored the three messages before each message.
- `embed-incremental` turned the message and its context into two vectors with a sentence model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). The model ran on the host's CPU and no message was sent anywhere. The model file itself came from the Hugging Face hub.
- `score-effort-v1` rated effort from 0 to 1 with a small linear model (`models/effort_v1.json`). It was trained on 2,774 of the 2,874 messages that Claude Haiku 4.5 labeled once.
- `score-quality` gave a rule-based score: length, variety, filler words.
- `score-resonance` counted the replies a message got.
- `score-substantiveness` combined novelty against the context, filler and word variety.
- `build-overlay-weekly` put the weekly averages into one small table.

The reader was the Quality tab of the dashboard, for moderators and up: averages over time, the spread of the effort scores, a ranking of authors and a weekly chart of ten metrics.

**Why it stopped.** It was taken out on purpose on 2026-05-15 (`62283076`). The database had grown to 9.4 GB and the integrity check at start hung the bot. That commit removed the Quality tab, the capture in the bot and migration 069, and its message says the tables had already been dropped on the host. On 2026-05-31 the embedding scripts left the repo too (#00089): their library, `@xenova/transformers`, had been deleted for a protobufjs vulnerability (#00003), and it fetched its model without a pinned version. The crontab on the host was changed neither time, so the seven jobs have run into the missing table ever since.

**What is on the host today.**

- The crontab has eight lines: the backup cleanup and the seven jobs. `scripts/quality-cron.txt` in the repo lists three of them.
- Each job stops at start with `SqliteError: no such table: general_messages_raw`. The 15-minute job has done so more than 17,600 times, which is 184 days of runs. The seven logs in `/tmp/quality-*.log` hold 44 MB.
- `general_messages_raw`, `_ctx`, `_embed`, `_label` and `_gold` are gone. Five score tables are there again, empty: each job creates its output table at start, before it reaches the missing one, so the dead jobs put them back after the drop. The indexes of migration 070 on those tables went with the drop and did not come back.
- The logs also hold 104 `database is locked` errors. All of them are from the two weeks in May when the jobs still did real work, in the same file the bot writes to.
- `user_names` is left over with 277 rows of display names.
- `scripts/embed-incremental.mjs`, `embed-all.mjs`, `embed-gold.mjs` and `migrations/069_general_messages_tables.ts` are still there, because a deploy unpacks over the folder and deletes nothing. `models/` holds both effort models; the deploy does not ship that folder.
- The embedding library was on the host without being in the lockfile. The clean install of the 26.0.0 deploy removed it on 2026-11-15: the last 22 runs of the embedding job, every one since that deploy, stopped at the import and no longer at the table.
- The machine has two CPUs and 7.8 GB of memory, 6.3 GB of it available, and 22 GB of disk free.

**What is on the dev machine.** The local `data/data.db` is 5.1 GB and holds the study as it was: 987,500 messages from 2025-09-30 to 2026-04-30 with their context, 945,625 pairs of vectors (3,072 bytes a message, 2.7 GB), the scores, and the 2,874 labeled messages. `scripts/README.md` calls the pipeline local only and says production carries the empty tables of migration 069. The second half is no longer true.

**What has changed since: the message archive has the input.** `messages_archive` holds the same channel from 2021-10-08 to today: 2,074,469 messages, 1,982,629 of them with text, 51.7 million characters, each with its reply target, bot flag, attachments and deleted flag. For the seven months of the old study it has 990,919 rows against the 987,500 of the old table. The bot keeps it current: 223,622 of the rows came in live. The channel gets about 1,000 messages a day now (29,582 in the last 30 days).

So the pipeline needs no table of its own for the text, no capture in the bot and no backfill from Discord.

**Where the 9.4 GB came from.** Almost all of it was stored input, not results: 3,072 bytes of vectors and about 270 characters of context for every message, next to a second copy of the text. For the whole channel the vectors alone would be 5.7 GB today. A score is a few bytes.

**The embedding step can come back.** Tried on the dev machine on 2026-11-15, in a scratch folder:

- `@huggingface/transformers` 4.3.2 is the maintained successor of the removed library. Its install has 42 packages and `npm audit` reports 0 vulnerabilities. It takes 470 MB on disk, most of it the ONNX runtime, so it belongs in a package of its own and not in the bot's dependencies.
- The model file is 22 MB. It can be kept on the host and pinned, with remote downloads switched off.
- 2,000 stored messages were embedded again and compared with their stored vectors. They are close, not equal: the median cosine is 0.987, the lowest 0.909. The effort score from the new vectors differs from the stored score by 0.012 on average, on its scale of 0 to 1 (99th percentile 0.043, largest 0.069). The model's own mean error against the labels is 0.085. From the stored vectors the scorer gives the stored scores exactly, so the difference comes from the runtime and not from the code.
- With two threads the dev machine did 116 messages a second. A day of the channel would take well under a minute on the host's two CPUs, even at half that speed. The process used about 510 MB of memory.
- Without embeddings only the rule-based score, the reply count and the older effort model are left. On its test set that model explains 32% of the variance of the labels, the embedding model 68%.

**The plan, if it comes back.** One job in place of seven, built so that the reason for the removal cannot return:

- It reads the channel from `messages_archive` and takes the context from there.
- It computes the vectors, uses them for the scores and throws them away. Only scores are stored, one row per message.
- The scores go into a file of their own, `data/research.db`. The bot's database, its backups, its replication and its start check stay as they are, and the job only reads the file the bot writes to. The estimate for all 2.07 million messages is 0.2 to 0.3 GB, to be measured before it ships.
- The code lives in a folder with its own `package.json` and lockfile, like `web/`, so the bot's install and its audit do not change.
- The history is scored once on the dev machine, from a copy of the archive and with one runtime for the whole series: about two hours with eight threads. The finished file is copied to the host. That run must not happen on the host, a burstable instance with two CPUs.
- On the host one cron line runs the job every 15 minutes. The seven old lines, the leftover scripts, the empty tables, `user_names` and the logs go.
- The Quality tab comes back from the history of the repo (`62283076^`), for moderators and up as before, and reads `data/research.db`. Author names come from the archive.
- No message leaves the host and no outside service is called. The labeling by Claude in April was a one-time step for training and is not repeated.
- A message that is deleted in Discord loses its score at the next run.

It would ship as 26.2.0: the job and its tests, the tab, the deploy steps for the new folder and the model, the docs. The bot's own code does not change.

**Not checked.**

- The speed on the host itself. The 116 messages a second are from the dev machine.
- The size of the score file. It is an estimate from row sizes.
- Whether the old Quality tab still fits the dashboard as it is six months later.
- The output of `score-substantiveness` and of the weekly table. The local copy has neither table, so there is nothing stored to compare a rebuilt version against.

**The owner's choices.**

- Scope. (a) Scores and the Quality tab, as planned above. (b) Scores only, kept current on the host, with no tab for now. (c) Not on the host after all: the seven lines and the leftovers are removed and the study stays on the dev machine.
- Embeddings. With the library on the host, which gives the effort score as before and makes substantiveness possible. Or without it, which leaves the rule-based score, the reply count and the weaker effort model.
- History. The whole channel since 2021, or from 2025-09-30 as before.
- The seven dead cron lines. They can be stopped now, whatever is chosen, because they do nothing but write errors. They stay until the owner says so.

The plan above is choice (a) with embeddings and the whole history.
